# CBL Examples & Case Studies

## Higher Education

**Loughborough University — Physics Curriculum**
A first-year computational project has students run numerical simulation of electromagnetic fields subject to temporal boundaries and time-domain metamaterials, deliberately introducing uncertainty and partial knowledge and requiring students to extend their understanding into unfamiliar contexts. As students progress through the degree, challenge scale and complexity increase, culminating in final-year projects where even defining the requirements becomes the students' own responsibility. This models a CBL principle: challenge ownership deepens over time as learners mature.

**Bioinstrumentation Blended Course**
A documented case of implementing a full CBL experience in a bioinstrumentation course — students moved through Engage/Investigate/Act to design instrumentation solutions, blending in-person and online learning modalities.

**Sustainability Education (Multi-Case Study, Secondary Schools)**
A 4-year partnership across **seven secondary schools, 26 teachers, and a university teacher-education program** used CBL as the primary approach for sustainability education. The study specifically examined how teachers design and enact *scaffolding* within CBL projects — i.e., how much structure vs. openness to give students at different points, a key facilitation question for any CBL implementation.

## K-12 / Classroom

**Apple Education Community Story**
"A Challenge Based Learning Example: How I Transformed My [Classroom]" — an educator account of adopting CBL to restructure their teaching approach around real challenges rather than fixed assignments.

**Game Design Course (Undergraduate Writing)**
An upper-level undergraduate writing course centered on game design had students create their own game design challenges — an example of CBL applied outside STEM, in a humanities/writing context, again showing the framework's subject-agnostic flexibility.

## Cross-Cutting Observations from the Case Studies

- CBL scales from single-classroom projects up to multi-year, multi-school institutional partnerships.
- It's used well beyond STEM — writing, physics, medicine, sustainability, game design, bioinstrumentation all appear as valid domains.
- **Scaffolding is a recurring design variable**: successful implementations tune how much structure/guidance is provided based on learner age/experience level, not a one-size-fits-all approach.
- Engagement and knowledge acquisition are consistently reported as improved outcomes: "Students are more engaged in the classroom when examining case studies, leading to greater knowledge acquisition," and CBL more broadly is reported to build "deeper understanding of course material and stronger critical thinking skills."
- Challenge complexity/openness should typically scale with learner maturity — early challenges can be more scaffolded/defined, later ones more student-owned (see Loughborough example).

## Implication for a Claude Skill

Since scaffolding level is a proven design variable, a CBL skill aimed at students building a product should probably ask early on: "How experienced is your team / how much structure do you want?" and adjust how much it defines the Challenge, Guiding Questions, and role assignments vs. leaving those fully open to the student team.
