# CBL Process — Practical Step-by-Step Guide

This breaks the three CBL phases into concrete, actionable steps a facilitator or a Claude skill could walk a student/team through.

## Phase 1: ENGAGE

Goal: turn an abstract theme into one concrete, actionable Challenge the team owns.

1. **Pick a Big Idea** — a broad theme with many angles, meaningful to the learner and their community (examples: Community, Relationships, Creativity, Health, Sustainability, Democracy).
2. **Run Essential Questioning** — ask personally-connecting questions about the Big Idea:
   - Why is this important to me?
   - Where does this concept intersect with my world?
   - What does this look like in my community?
   Narrow this down to **one Essential Question** with real contextual meaning to the learner.
3. **Convert the Essential Question into a Challenge statement** — a call to action that is:
   - Actionable (something can actually be *done*, not just studied)
   - Specific enough to build excitement and focus
   - Open enough to allow multiple solution paths
   - Example pattern: "Design/Create/Develop a ___ that ___ for ___."

**Output of Engage**: one written Challenge statement the team can rally behind.

## Phase 2: INVESTIGATE

Goal: build the knowledge foundation needed to propose a credible, informed solution.

1. **Generate Guiding Questions** — brainstorm everything you'd need to know to solve the Challenge. Cast a wide net first.
2. **Categorize & prioritize** the questions — group into themes (e.g., user needs, technical constraints, existing solutions, market/context) and rank by what's most critical to answer first. This becomes the research roadmap.
3. **Identify Guiding Activities & Resources** for each question:
   - Resources: articles, courses, databases, textbooks, interviews, expert/social networks
   - Activities: experiments, simulations, surveys, user interviews, competitive research, problem sets, prototyping exercises
4. **Carry out the research** — work through guiding questions systematically, documenting findings as you go.
5. **Synthesize** — analyze everything gathered, identify patterns/themes, and draw clear conclusions.
6. **Produce a synthesis output** — a short report or presentation showing the Guiding Questions have been answered and there's a solid, evidence-based foundation for a solution.

**Output of Investigate**: a synthesis report/presentation + a validated foundation of knowledge to build a solution on.

## Phase 3: ACT

Goal: design, build, implement, and evaluate a real solution.

1. **Develop Solution Concepts** — brainstorm multiple possible solutions grounded in the Investigate findings. Types include: awareness/education campaigns, school/community improvement projects, **product development**, services, or other interventions.
2. **Select and scope a solution concept** — converge on one (or a small number) worth pursuing, and get it approved/validated (by a teacher, mentor, or stakeholder).
3. **Build & iterate**:
   - Develop a prototype
   - Experiment and test it
   - This iterative cycle often surfaces new Guiding Questions — loop back to Investigate as needed
   - Refine based on feedback
4. **Implement** the solution with a real, authentic audience (not just a classroom demo).
5. **Measure outcomes** — define what success looks like up front, then measure actual impact against it.
6. **Reflect** — what worked, what didn't, why.
7. **Close out**: either continue refining the solution, or produce a completion report and share the work publicly.

**Output of Act**: an implemented (not just theoretical) solution, evaluated results, and a shareable account of impact.

## The Thread Underneath: Reflect, Document, Share

This isn't a fourth phase — it runs continuously across all three phases:

- Document as you go using text, audio, video, images/photos.
- Use this running documentation as raw material for reflection and for informal/formative assessment.
- Share progress and final results publicly (blog, video, presentation, community showcase) — sharing is part of the learning, not just a wrap-up formality.

## Non-Linearity Note

CBL is drawn as a circular/looping process, not a straight line. Expect:
- Mini exploration cycles inside any phase
- Jumping back from Act to Investigate when prototyping reveals new unknowns
- Revisiting Engage if the Challenge itself turns out to be mis-scoped

A good facilitation skill should treat "going back a phase" as a normal, healthy part of the process — not a failure.
