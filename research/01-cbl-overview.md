# Challenge Based Learning (CBL) — Overview & Framework

## What is CBL?

Challenge Based Learning (CBL) is a framework for learning while solving real-world challenges. It is collaborative and hands-on, asking all participants — students, teachers, and community members — to:

- identify big ideas
- ask good questions
- discover and solve a genuine challenge
- gain in-depth subject-area knowledge
- develop 21st-century skills (collaboration, critical thinking, communication, creativity)
- share their thinking and solutions with the world

Unlike traditional project-based learning, CBL starts from a broad, open-ended "Big Idea" rather than a pre-packaged project brief, and pushes learners to define their own actionable challenge before researching and building a solution.

## Origins

- Created by a team at **Apple in 2008**, originally to help schools use technology in more meaningful, relevant ways.
- In 2016, the **Digital Promise** team and the founding CBL team jointly updated and re-released the CBL content/guide.
- Today CBL is used by K-12 schools, universities, and organizations worldwide across math, science, social studies, language arts, medicine, technology, engineering, computer science, and the arts.
- Digital Promise now offers consulting/professional services to help schools implement the framework.

## Core Structure: Three Phases

CBL is organized into three interconnected phases, with **documentation, reflection, and sharing** running underneath the entire process:

1. **Engage** — move from an abstract Big Idea to a concrete, personally meaningful, actionable Challenge.
2. **Investigate** — research deeply to build the knowledge foundation needed for a credible solution.
3. **Act** — design, prototype, implement, and evaluate a real solution with an authentic audience.

The process is **not strictly linear**. Each phase contains "mini-exploration cycles," and learners often loop back to an earlier phase (e.g., Act frequently kicks off new Guiding Questions that send the team back into Investigate).

## Why It Matters / Core Principles

- **Authenticity**: challenges are real, personally and locally/globally relevant — not hypothetical.
- **Learner ownership**: students choose and shape their own challenge and questions rather than receiving a fixed assignment.
- **Content + skill integration**: deep subject-matter learning is acquired *in service of* solving the challenge, not as a separate track.
- **Public, real audience**: solutions are implemented and shared outward, not just submitted for a grade.
- **Iterative**: research, design, and implementation loop back on each other; failure and revision are expected.
- **Reflection as backbone**: ongoing documentation and reflection are treated as core learning activities, not an afterthought.

## Relevance to Building a Product-Focused CBL Skill

For a skill meant to help students build a product, CBL is a strong scaffold because its **Act phase explicitly names "product development"** as a valid Solution Concept, and its iterative Solution Development step (prototype → test → refine) maps directly onto product-building cycles (see `04-cbl-product-building.md`).
